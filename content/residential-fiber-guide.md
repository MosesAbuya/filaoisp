## What Makes Optical Fiber Superior to Copper and 4G Wireless for Home Internet?

Modern households in Kenya connect an average of 8 to 15 internet-enabled devices simultaneously, including smartphones, smart televisions, laptops, and smart home appliances. According to the Communications Authority of Kenya (CAK) 2024 telecom report, fixed fiber optic subscriptions in Kenya expanded by over 28% year-over-year as consumers transitioned away from copper ADSL and congested cellular 4G routers. 

Optical fiber transmits data pulses of light across ultra-pure silicon glass strands. Because glass conductors do not conduct electrical current, optical fiber suffers zero electromagnetic interference from power lines, lightning storms, or radio frequencies. Symmetrical bandwidth ensures that file upload speeds match your download speeds, providing flawless Zoom video conferences and rapid cloud file synchronizations.

## How Does Symmetrical Bandwidth Prevent Buffering During Peak Household Usage Hours?

In conventional broadband arrangements, internet service providers allocate 80% to 90% of overall capacity solely to downloads. While adequate for basic static web page reading, modern smart homes place enormous strain on the reverse upstream channel. When a single family member uploads a video to YouTube, backs up Google Photos, or streams on TikTok, an asymmetrical network saturates, triggering packet drops and buffering across all connected devices.

Filao Networks provisions true symmetrical capacity across our Fiber-to-the-Home (FTTH) network. Fiber optic attenuation remains below 0.2 dB per kilometer across single-mode fiber backbones, enabling continuous gigabit data transfer without packet degradation. Symmetrical throughput means multiple 4K HDR streams—each requiring 25 Mbps of sustained bit rate according to Netflix and YouTube encoding standards—run simultaneously with zero stutter or resolution drops.
