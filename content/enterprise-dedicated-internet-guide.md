## What Distinguishes Dedicated Internet Access From Shared Commercial Broadband?

For businesses running enterprise resource planning (ERP) software, hosted VoIP telephone networks, fintech payment gateways, and database clusters, connectivity reliability directly impacts bottom-line revenue. The World Bank estimates that unplanned digital infrastructure disruptions cost East African financial institutions and corporations over $2.5 million annually in lost transactions and employee productivity.

Shared commercial broadband operates on oversubscription ratios between 1:10 and 1:30, meaning your business shares a single bandwidth pipe with dozens of neighboring consumers. During peak business hours (9:00 AM to 4:00 PM), shared throughput plunges drastically. In contrast, Filao Networks Dedicated Internet Access guarantees an uncontended 1:1 ratio: 100 Mbps ordered equates to 100 Mbps reserved exclusively on our optical core, 24 hours a day, 365 days a year.

## How Does Direct Local Peering with KIXP Optimize Enterprise Latency?

When your corporate branches communicate with Kenyan cloud services, local banks, or AWS/Azure edge caches, traffic should never route through undersea cables to Europe and back. Filao Networks maintains direct gigabit peering at the Kenya Internet Exchange Point (KIXP) in Nairobi and Mombasa.

Local peering slashes round-trip packet latency from 180 milliseconds (the round-trip delay to London and back) down to less than 4 milliseconds within Kenya. This latency reduction accelerates real-time video conferencing, eliminates VoIP jitter, and ensures rapid database synchronizations between headquarters and branch offices.
